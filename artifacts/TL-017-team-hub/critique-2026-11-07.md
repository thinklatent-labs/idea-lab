## Feasibility seat
**Evidence so far.** Every hypothesis rests on simulation or string checks, not on people:
- **H1:** what was measured is hook latency (0.12 to 0.19 s). That is not how long a message waits for the recipient's next prompt.
- **H1b (idle wake):** no evidence. It depends on an undocumented socket wire format (`daemon.py:102-108`, `CORTEX_WAKE_FORMAT`) and has no Windows path.
- **H2:** the framing holds at string level. How the model behaves on Bedrock is untested.
- **H6:** routing passes in the simulation.

No Claude Code session on Bedrock has ever run the hooks. None of the kill criteria can have fired, because none has been measured.

**Pre-mortem:**
1. Nobody adopted it: 0.1 to 0.8 were built in two days with no users.
2. The hooks slowed every session: a fresh `python3` starts on every tool call, and `api.try_call` has no circuit breaker.
3. Idle wake never worked.
4. Enterprise approvals for SSO, hosting, InfoSec, managed settings and Jira stalled it.
5. The process overhead (gates, one-writer specs, ADRs) turned people away.

**Scope.**
- Keep: messaging, presence, hand-off, claims, framing, `cortex doctor`.
- Freeze for the pilot, off by default: rooms, specs, co-editing, the design gate, flows, loops, forecasts, ask and cite-check, detectors, multi-node Postgres, and the console beyond inbox and presence.

**Second-order effects:**
- support load on laptops;
- endpoint-security flags;
- unstaffed curator roles;
- surveillance perception of the active-time metrics;
- the lab's own gate bypassed while the row sat at Seed.

## Risk seat: findings
High findings, all to be fixed before any pilot:
1. **The sender of a peer message can be faked.** `from_session` is taken from the request body (`store.py:261`) and shown as `from=` in the framing.
2. **Claim text reaches Claude unframed, in Cortex's own voice.** Claim path and session go into PostToolUse and PreToolUse unframed (`hooks.py:211`, `hooks.py:251`), straight from the claimer's request body.
3. **The privacy promises are false.**
   - The State and Next of a structured hand-off can be read by the whole team (`events.py:178-183`), although the console says sender and recipient only.
   - Items and decisions from invite-only rooms leak into team events and into ask.
   - The text of group messages goes into team summaries.
4. **There is no real authentication.** OIDC is a stub, `dev-oidc` accepts unsigned tokens, there are static tokens in docker-compose, and the server speaks plain HTTP.
5. **The co-editing sidecar's secret defaults to `change-me`.** It is accepted on the public port, and tokens travel over `ws://`.

Medium findings:
- group membership is not checked, so anyone can join any group by naming a session;
- redaction misses hand-offs, ADRs, specs, plans and loop commands, and its patterns are narrow;
- nothing is ever deleted;
- an unreachable hub adds seconds to every prompt and edit;
- the Stop hook runs the loop check with `shell=True`, and repeated urgent messages can block Stop;
- ask has no rate limit, and server-side Bedrock uses the server's role;
- releases are unpinned and unsigned, and no human reviews the hooks.

Low findings: the console token is kept in sessionStorage (the CSP is strict), link fields are not validated, and the daemon socket is chmod-ed after bind.

**Framing.** Framing is advisory. The only injection test checks escaping, not model behaviour. The real controls are permission modes, the tools allowed, and human approval before any action.

**Simulated InfoSec pre-review** (simulated, not a sign-off). InfoSec would ask for:
- OIDC and TLS, plus a pen test;
- a data-flow diagram and classification, a retention policy and a DPIA;
- an injection threat model tested against the live model;
- plugin signing, pinned versions and a kill switch;
- Bedrock identity and a cost ceiling;
- hosting and an incident owner;
- an SBOM for the sidecar.

## What 0.9 becomes (D-166)
0.9 is pilot hardening and readiness, with a feature freeze. The `docs/backlog.md` file in the cortex repo holds the items:
- C-14 to C-21 come from the first pass: the readiness pack, hook budget, urgent no longer blocking Stop, retention, the pilot scope switches, safer defaults, pinned source and a live session test.
- C-22 to C-29 come from this pass: sender identity, framing every peer string, the privacy promises, transport and secrets, membership checks, redaction on every field, Stop and loop limits, and a circuit breaker with a kill switch.

No pilot runs while any of C-22 to C-25 is open. The Enterprise's real InfoSec and IT sign-off is outside the lab: it gates the pilot inside the Enterprise, not the lab stage.

## Kill signals (dated)
- H1 median over 30 s, or any H2 injection acted on, in the pilot's first week: stop the pilot, and park the messaging route.
- Fewer than 2 of the 3 to 5 volunteers use it weekly by week 3 of the pilot: park.
- Any of the five high findings still open at the pilot date: no pilot. The appetite clock keeps running.
- The appetite (4 engineer-weeks, ending 2026-12-05) runs out without an H1 and H2 reading from real sessions: stop, and go back to Stress test.
