# Specialists win tasks, frontier models win domains

**Bottom line (as of 2 November 2026).** Across advice, compliance, cybersecurity and evaluation, the enterprises and vendors that actually own trained weights are almost never the regulated firms themselves: they are data vendors, AI vendors, security vendors and research labs, and their models are tuned for narrow, repeatable tasks (classification, extraction, mapping, scoring, judging), not for open-ended domain reasoning. Every production advice tool found at a regulated firm (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Vanguard, Betterment, Fidelity, LPL/Orion) is a frontier model plus retrieval over house content; no bank or wealth manager discloses tuned weights for advice ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/); [Vanguard, 9 Apr 2026](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/who-we-are/pressroom/press-release-vanguard-launches-expert-insights-equipping-advisors-with-ai-powered-portfolio-analysis-expertise-04092026.html)). The evidence from 2023–2026 is consistent: a tuned 1–32B model reliably beats a frontier generalist only when the task is narrow and stable, the output is structured, there are at least ~1K in-distribution examples, volume is high, or the specialist sees proprietary content the frontier cannot; it reliably loses on broad reasoning, and the frontier catches up within 6–12 months (BloombergGPT → GPT-4 in 2023, Med-PaLM 2 → GPT-4+Medprompt, Thomson-1-Large still behind Gemini 3.1 Pro on LegalBench in Aug 2026) ([Li et al., Oct 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05862); [LawSites, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/08/thomson-reuters-says-its-homegrown-ai-model-now-rivals-the-frontier-labs-i-take-a-closer-look.html)). Published results put the "enough" size at **0.6–8B** for every horizontal task primitive (judging, grounding, safety, PII, routing, extraction, ASR), with no published gain beyond 8B ([Skywork-V2, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/d8836d0294651a867c2ca51012923997-Paper-Conference.pdf)). For the Head of VI, who already owns a ~27B domain-adapted Core, a Distil tier and a planned Domain tier, this implies the Domain tier should be built as **task-adapter families on the existing bases, not as separately pretrained domain bases**: Judge and Compliance first (narrow, structured, data-rich, reusable by every other application), Cybersecurity second (reuse open 8B security models and adapters, do not pretrain), and Advice last and only as narrative, summarisation and evaluation adapters behind a deterministic suitability layer, because the evidence argues against a trained-weight "Advice" model on quality, regulatory and precedent grounds. The research could not close several gaps: no independent benchmark of any vendor copilot, no advice-quality benchmark anywhere, no controlled study of a shared-base adapter family against separate models, and all enterprise cost figures are company-reported.

## The trained-weights line follows data ownership, not ambition

The single most useful finding for a Head of Enterprise AI deciding what to build is where the "trained weights" line actually falls in the market. It does not fall between ambitious and cautious firms. It falls between **firms whose edge is a high-volume proprietary data stream** (payments, telemetry, labelled surveillance alerts, case law) and **firms whose edge is documents and judgment**. The first group trains weights because no vendor has their data; the second group buys a frontier model, wraps it in retrieval over house content, and keeps the regulated decision with a human or a pre-approved algorithm.

In financial services the pattern is stark. The firms that train their own foundation models at scale do it on transaction or tabular sequence data, not text: Stripe's payments foundation model trained on "tens of billions of transactions" (May 2025, firm-claimed 64% lift in card-testing detection) ([TechCrunch, 7 May 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/07/stripe-unveils-ai-foundation-model-for-payments-reveals-deeper-partnership-with-nvidia)); Mastercard's Large Tabular Model trained on "billions of anonymized payment transactions" and explicitly intended to reduce the number of models it maintains (Apr 2026) ([Built In, 28 Apr 2026](https://builtin.com/articles/what-its-build-generative-ai-models-mastercard)); Nubank's "nuFormer" at billion-parameter scale with a conservatively reported **+1.20% average AUC lift** over gradient-boosted baselines (Jun 2025) ([Nubank engineering, 11 Jun 2025](https://building.nu.com/foundation-models-ai-nubank-transformation/)). Western banks with document- and advice-shaped businesses (JPMorgan, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Wells Fargo, UBS, Deutsche, BNP, Allianz) overwhelmingly orchestrate 2–4 frontier vendors plus one open-weight family behind an in-house gateway, reserving in-house training for small task models: Wells Fargo runs an in-house small model for PII detection and lets Gemini Flash handle intent only ([VentureBeat, 8 Apr 2025](https://www.venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm)); JPMorgan's LLM Suite swaps GPT-4-class models in and out and notes that "small models can be fine-tuned" as a capability, not a disclosed deployment ([American Banker, 22 May 2025](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/how-jpmorganchase-democratized-employee-access-to-gen-ai)); Capital One customised Llama as the base for its customer-facing Chat Concierge while keeping credit decisions on traditional ML ([Euromoney, 27 Feb 2025](https://www.euromoney.com/article/2eh2s01l11023kmxtleyo/fintech/prem-natarajan-on-capital-ones-ai-stairway-to-heaven/)). The canonical retreat is Bloomberg: its 50B BloombergGPT, trained in 2023 on ~363B tokens of proprietary financial text, "was a research model and is actually not used at all in any of our products" (Apr 2026); AskB now runs on "a mix of commercial models, open-weight LLMs, and smaller custom models" ([A-Team Insight, Apr 2026](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/inside-bloombergs-askb-roadmap-the-data-moat-behind-the-ai/?brand=ati)). The exception is China, where ICBC, Ping An, Ant Group and Du Xiaoman train or heavily post-train their own finance LLMs on domestic open bases (Qwen, DeepSeek, Seed-OSS), driven by sovereignty constraints ([Ping An, Mar 2026](https://group.pingan.com/media/news/2026/pingans-financial-llm-ranks-first-in-cnfinbench-evaluation.html)); even there, insiders reported in May 2025 that in-house bank LLMs were falling short on returns and that the "one monolithic model" idea is "clearly flawed" ([Yicai Global, 23 May 2025](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/industry-insiders-regard-chinas-banking-ai-llms-unsatisfactory)).

### Advice: nobody with a licence has trained an advice model

The advice domain has the clearest answer. Every production advisor or client tool identified is a frontier model plus retrieval and prompting over proprietary content, and the regulated recommendation is kept inside existing methodology or advisor logic with the model acting as an explanation layer. Morgan Stanley's Assistant and Debrief are "embedded GPT-4" and Whisper with OpenAI-tuned retrieval over a 100,000-document corpus, used by over 98% of advisor teams and credited with lifting document access from 20% to 80% (firm claim via vendor case study) ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)). Vanguard's Expert Insights (9 Apr 2026) "combines Vanguard's portfolio expertise with generative AI" to assess advisor portfolios against Vanguard methodology, with no model or vendor disclosed and a stated position that the human advisor remains essential for assessing, communicating and deciding ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/fintech-bytes-vanguard-gives-advisors-an-ai-portfolio-analyst-with-expert-insights-launch/266081)). Betterment's Account Recommender (Mar 2026) pairs "advisor logic with AI-generated explanations" ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/265952-betterment-launches-ai-enabled-account-solution-to-streamline-investing-and-savings/)); BlackRock's Aladdin Wealth Auto Commentary (Oct 2025) turns risk analytics and the house CIO view into talking points and has since been ported from Morgan Stanley to Wells Fargo's Advisor Gateway (May 2026) ([InvestmentNews, Oct 2025](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/blackrock-debuts-ai-powered-commentary-tool-for-advisors-lands-morgan-stanley-as-first-client/262370)); Anthropic's wealth-management plug-ins (Feb 2026) were adopted by LPL's 30,000+ advisors and integrated into Orion ([InvestmentNews, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.investmentnews.com/transformation/lpl-orion-add-anthropics-new-ai-for-advisors/265438)). Robinhood Cortex and Fidelity International's Freya disclaim advice entirely ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/cortex); [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/fidelity-personal-investing-launches-freya-a-conversational-ai-interface-to-support-customers-needs/)).

Trained-weight finance models do exist, but every one is tuned for finance NLP, exam reasoning or document QA, not for personalised advice, and every one is owned by a data vendor, AI vendor, Chinese fintech or academic group: BloombergGPT (50B, 2023, never released) ([arXiv 2303.17564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17564)); Writer's Palmyra-Fin-70B-32K (Jul 2024, vendor-claimed 73% on a CFA Level III sample) ([Writer](https://writer.com/engineering/palmyra-med-fin-models)); Fin-R1 (7B on Qwen2.5, SFT then GRPO on 60K bilingual chain-of-thought items, Mar 2025) ([arXiv 2503.16252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16252)); Ant Group's Agentar-Fin-R1 (8B/32B on Qwen3, Jul 2025) ([arXiv 2507.16802](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16802v3)); Du Xiaoman's XuanYuan 4.0 (36B on Seed-OSS, early 2026) ([arXiv 2602.22273](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22273v1)). The regulatory environment explains the absence. No regulator has written AI-specific advice rules; FINRA 24-09 (Jun 2024) treats AI-generated client communications as regulated communications needing supervision and records ([FINRA 24-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-09)), ESMA (May 2024) requires that AI recommendations match each client's situation and that management bodies remain responsible ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf)), and the SEC's 2026 exam priorities check that algorithms produce advice "consistent with investors' stated strategies" ([Harvard Law Forum, 4 Jan 2026](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/01/04/2026-sec-division-of-examinations-priorities/)). The SEC's first AI-washing actions (Mar 2024) fined a firm that called itself the "first regulated AI financial advisor" ([SEC 2024-36](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-36)), and Robinhood's chief legal officer told a FINRA conference in May 2026 that generative advice tools "could breach existing securities rules" ([Rallies.ai](https://rallies.ai/news/robinhood-legal-chief-warns-ai-investment-advice-may-breach-regulation-best-interest)). The practical consequence is that per-client suitability is enforced by a deterministic layer or a human, never by generative weights, which removes the main reason to train an "advice" model at all.

### Compliance: trained weights live with whoever owns the labels

In compliance the trained-weight column is populated by vendors with a disclosed proprietary labelled dataset and by open legal research models; banks orchestrate. Behavox trains a proprietary LLM on regulated financial communications curated by 200+ compliance professionals and claims a further 60% alert reduction on top of 80% from rules, with true-positive rates above 90% (vendor claim, Sep 2025) ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/behavoxs-case-for-explainable-ai-in-compliance-trust-not-magic/)); Fidelity's Saifr trains NLP models on "millions" of compliance-reviewed marketing documents to flag, explain and rewrite risky language (May 2023, pre-2024) ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230515005854/en/Saifr-Introduces-Risk-Interpretation-and-Suggested-Language-AI-Models-to-Accelerate-Compliant-Content-Creation)); Luminance's Luna Crescent (Jun 2026) is fine-tuned on part of a 220M-document legal set and claims +5% over general models on an internal benchmark (vendor claim) ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/luminance-launches-luna-crescent-contract-ai-model)); Harvey found API fine-tuning and RAG "limiting" and built a custom case-law model with OpenAI that lawyers preferred 97% of the time over GPT-4 (vendor claim, 2023–24) ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey)). On the research side, SaulLM-7B/54B/141B (Mar–Jul 2024) continued-pretrained Mistral and Mixtral on 30B–540B legal tokens ([arXiv 2403.03883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03883); [arXiv 2407.19584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19584)), and Lawma (ICLR 2025) showed lightly fine-tuned small models beating GPT-4.5 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet on 260 legal annotation tasks with "a few hundred to a thousand labeled examples" each ([arXiv 2407.16615](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2407.16615)).

The production systems that financial institutions actually cite are either supervised non-LLM machine learning or frontier models behind agents. The only bank-attributed compliance number with a described methodology is HSBC's Google-built AML AI: 60% fewer alerts and 2–4x more suspicious activity found over 1.2 billion transactions a month (Nov 2023, pre-2024; still described as "machine learning capability", not an LLM, in Oct 2025) ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/financial-services/how-hsbc-fights-money-launderers-with-artificial-intelligence); [AML Intelligence, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2025/10/insight-how-google-ai-tech-helped-hsbc-revolutionize-its-aml-system/)). A major UK bank's LLM framework for complaint-resolution letters reported a 62% drafting-time reduction and tens of thousands of letters since Nov 2024, with no fine-tuning stated ([NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/132535)). Surveillance and sanctions vendors (NICE Actimize, Smarsh, Shield, Nasdaq Verafin) report 60–85% false-positive reductions and >80% workload cuts, none naming an underlying model and all vendor-claimed ([NICE Actimize, 28 May 2025](https://www.niceactimize.com/press-releases/nice-actimize-empowers-surveil-x-with-generative-ai-479/); [Nasdaq IR, 21 Jul 2025](https://ir.nasdaq.com/node/109326/pdf); [CPA Practice Advisor, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/06/29/shield-introduces-the-first-governed-ai-agent-designed-to-close-compliance-alerts-autonomously/185838/)). The governance context has shifted: US regulators replaced SR 11-7 with SR 26-2 on 17 April 2026 and expressly carved generative and agentic AI out of its scope, leaving banks to apply "analogous governance" while an interagency request for information is pending ([CRA Insights, Jul 2026](https://media.crai.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/09165122/FE-Insights-MRM-Guidance-SR-26-2-In-the-Era-of-AI-July2026.pdf)); a 44-bank Risk.net study found only about one in five formally monitor the effectiveness of their human-in-the-loop controls ([Risk.net, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.risk.net/benchmarking/model-risk/7963817/few-banks-formally-evaluate-genai-human-in-the-loop-controls)), and Bank of England and FSB officials have argued that human review of individual outputs is not adequate risk management on its own ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/risk-management/7963553/regulators-question-human-in-the-loop-as-ai-governance-tool)). Vendor benchmark withdrawals are a negative signal: Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis declined to take part in the Oct 2025 Vals legal benchmark and vLex withdrew before publication ([LawSites, Oct 2025](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/10/vals-ais-latest-benchmark-finds-legal-and-general-ai-now-outperform-lawyers-in-legal-research-accuracy.html)).

### Cybersecurity: open 8B security models exist; vendor copilots are frontier plus telemetry

Cybersecurity is the one domain with reproducible, openly published security-trained models, and they cluster at 8B. Cisco's Foundation-Sec-8B (Apr 2025) continued-pretrained Llama-3.1-8B on ~5.1B tokens distilled from a 4 TiB security crawl; its Instruct (Aug 2025), 1.1 (Nov 2025, 64K context) and Reasoning (Jan 2026, 2M synthetic exemplars plus GRPO) variants followed, and Cisco reports production use in Duo identity digests for 2,000+ customers, SoftBank's SOC and Deloitte Japan ([arXiv 2504.21039](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21039v1); [arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051v1); [The Register, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/17/cisco_foundation_model_indentity_intelligence/)). Trend Micro's Llama-Primus family (Feb 2025) continued-pretrained ~2.76B tokens on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and released its datasets under ODC-BY ([arXiv 2502.11191](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11191v2)); Dream's MiST (EMNLP 2026) showed that synthetic "mid-training" on ~1B tokens expanded from a 65M-token vetted seed beats raw continual pretraining on 2.4B tokens, lifting Qwen3-8B's mean cyber score from 48.6 to 61.7 and Qwen3-32B from 54.6 to 63.2 ([arXiv 2609.18496](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18496)). Every major SOC copilot, by contrast, is a frontier general model wrapped in retrieval, orchestration and proprietary telemetry: Microsoft Security Copilot runs on GPT-4/4.1 ([Infosecurity Magazine, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/interviews/exclusive-microsoft-security)); Google describes SecLM as "a platform, not a fine-tuned model" ([Google Cloud Security Community, 12 Jun 2025](https://security.googlecloudcommunity.com/community-blog-42/fueling-ai-innovation-in-secops-products-the-seclm-platform-and-sec-gemini-research-pipeline-3997)); Palo Alto lets SOC teams pick among Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.8 and Gemini 3.5 Flash per task (Jul 2026) ([Palo Alto Networks](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/blog/security-operations/whats-new-in-cortex-july-2026.md/)); CrowdStrike's Charlotte triage is a trained triage agent on "millions" of human MDR decisions over unnamed LLMs ([CrowdStrike, 13 Feb 2025](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/agentic-ai-innovation-in-cybersecurity-charlotte-ai-detection-triage/)). No bank, insurer or asset manager was found publicly claiming to have trained its own security LLM; UK banks are instead taking access to OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Cyber (Jun 2026) ([Pulse, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/openai-expands-cybersecurity-ai-access-to-major-uk-banks-cdfedfa4-9b4f-4a8a-a1db-c074b9423ceb/)), and the one on-record financial-institution SOC practitioner (Exeter Finance, RSAC Mar 2026) reports the value in summarisation and evidence stitching, "not in the autonomous response", under a "no gate, no action" policy ([American Banker, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/how-banks-can-safely-use-ai-in-the-cyber-operations-center)).

### Judges and horizontal tasks: the frontier has moved to 8B and below

For evaluation and the other horizontal primitives, the picture inverts: by late 2026 every horizontal task has at least one open-weight model in the 0.1–8B range that matches or beats GPT-4o-class models on the task's own benchmark, while generalist judging of correctness and out-of-distribution robustness still favour frontier reasoning models. Skywork-Reward V2 (ICLR 2026) trains Bradley-Terry reward models from 0.6B to 8B on 40M curated preference pairs; its **1.7B model (75.2 average over seven benchmarks) beats a 70B reward model (73.8)**, its 0.6B matches GPT-4o (70.9 vs 70.4), and its 8B reaches 85.8–88.6 ([Skywork-V2](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/d8836d0294651a867c2ca51012923997-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Patronus Lynx-8B reaches 82.9% on HaluBench against GPT-4o's 86.5% from 2,400 labelled RAG examples ([arXiv 2407.08488](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08488v2)); a 4B HalluGuard matches GPT-4o on LLM-AggreFact (75.7 vs 75.9) ([arXiv 2510.00880](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00880)). In an independent 14-model guardrail benchmark (Apr 2026), F1 clusters at 0.72–0.76 for the best 4–8B models and size does not predict performance (r=0.21, p=0.48); Llama Guard 4 at 12B scores 0.468 ([arXiv 2605.28830](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28830v1)). The limit is equally clear: on JudgeBench's objective-correctness tasks, Prometheus 2 scores 40% (random is 50%) against o3-mini-high's 81% ([Beri, 25 Oct 2026](https://www.beri.net/article/best-llm-as-a-judge-model-reasoning-vs-fine-tuned-judges-calibration-cost-2026)), and an ACL 2025 study concluded fine-tuned judges become "task-specific classifiers" that score below chance on adversarial subsets and "cannot replace GPT-4 as a universal evaluator" ([Huang et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.306.pdf)). Financial-services adoption of these primitives is documented but metric-free: JPMorgan's FENCE builds use-case-specific guardrails from synthetic data and is in production for Q&A and search (Apr 2026, no metrics) ([JPMC](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/fence-framework)); Česká spořitelna runs Whisper plus internal GPT and Mixtral over ~2M calls a year ([Databricks, 18 Dec 2024](https://www.databricks.com/blog/ceska-erste-genai)); Morgan Stanley relies on advisor and prompt-engineer graders with a daily regression suite rather than model judges ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)).

## The evidence draws one line: narrow and structured beats broad and open

The two bodies of evidence that look contradictory, "specialists beat frontier" and "frontier beats specialists", measure different things and are both right. Frontier models win **domain-generalist** comparisons; specialists win **task** comparisons; and the gap between a strong open ~27B and the best frontier model on broad domain benchmarks is now roughly the size of the gain that heavy domain pretraining used to buy.

On the domain-generalist side the record is unbroken. GPT-4 beat BloombergGPT on FiQA sentiment (88.1 vs 75.1), headline classification (86.0 vs 82.2) and ConvFinQA (76.5 vs 43.4) within months of its release, and small fine-tuned task models beat both on NER and headlines (Oct 2023) ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05862)). A 7B model with the right continued-pretraining recipe matched the 50B BloombergGPT on finance prompting (63.4 vs 62.5) using 1.2B tokens (ICLR 2024) ([AdaptLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09530.pdf)). GPT-4 with prompting alone exceeded the fine-tuned Med-PaLM 2 on MedQA (90.2% vs 86.5%, Nov 2023) ([Medprompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16452v1)), and Google's own MedGemma 27B, after medical post-training that added 12.8 points, still sits below Gemini 2.5 Pro and o3 while losing 7.3 points on MMLU-Pro (Apr 2026) ([MedGemma TR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05201)). Thomson Reuters' Thomson-1-Large (31 Jul 2026), built from an open base plus mid- and post-training on Westlaw and Practical Law, self-reports LegalBench 0.823 against Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.843 and GPT-5.5 0.832, winning three of seven rows and only where the frontier models were denied Westlaw access ([LawSites, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/08/thomson-reuters-says-its-homegrown-ai-model-now-rivals-the-frontier-labs-i-take-a-closer-look.html)). On the Vals LegalBench leaderboard (13 Oct 2026) frontier models cluster at 87–89% while Qwen 3.8 27B scores 82.4%; the ~6-point gap is roughly what SaulLM-141B obtained from 540B legal tokens in 2024 ([Vals AI](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legalbench); [Equall](https://blog.equall.com/saul)). Cisco's Foundation-Sec-8B lost 2.4 MMLU points and trails Llama-3.3-70B by 5–15 points on broad security knowledge even after reasoning post-training ([arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051v1)).

On the task side the record is equally consistent. LoRA Land (May 2024, vendor-affiliated) fine-tuned 310 ≤7B models on 31 tasks: **72% beat GPT-4** on their task, by 9.5 points on average for the best per task, while GPT-4 kept winning on broad tasks like coding and MMLU ([arXiv 2405.00732](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00732v1)). Shopify's fine-tuned Qwen3-32B tool-calling agent is "2.2x faster and 68% cheaper" than the frontier model it replaced and serves the majority of production traffic after a weekly retrain flywheel closed an initial 35% quality gap in two weeks (company-reported, Apr 2026) ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/fine-tuning-agent-shopify-flow)). LinkedIn's EON-8B gained 4 points over GPT-4o mini on candidate matching at a claimed 75x lower cost than GPT-4 ([ZenML/LinkedIn](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/domain-adapted-foundation-models-for-enterprise-scale-llm-deployment)). Pinterest rejected an 8B LLM for its relevance model because a 6x cost increase bought only a "slight" gain over a fine-tuned encoder (RecSys 2025) ([arXiv 2509.03764](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03764v2)). A fine-tuned Llama-2-7B on 2,331 call-centre recordings matched GPT-4 on factual validity and completeness of call summaries ([Thulke et al., 2024](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/download/1268/Thulke-FLLM-2024.pdf)). Lawma's few-hundred-example fine-tunes beat GPT-4.5 on legal annotation ([arXiv 2407.16615](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2407.16615)).

The recurring condition set is: a narrow and stable task; a fixed or structured output (labels, JSON, DSL, tool calls, scores); at least ~1K high-quality in-distribution examples, ideally with a production flywheel; high volume; or proprietary content the frontier model cannot see. Absent those, the prompting-the-frontier evidence wins, and the market agrees: OpenAI is winding down its self-serve fine-tuning platform (announced 7 May 2026, final job creation 6 Jan 2027), citing that newer base models "are much better at following instructions and formats" ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations.md); [Tessl, 20 May 2026](https://tessl.io/blog/openai-shutting-fine-tuning-signals-for-enterprise-ai)); Menlo Ventures' Dec 2025 survey finds prompt design and RAG dominant and fine-tuning "still niche", with 76% of enterprise AI use cases bought rather than built ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/menlo_ventures_enterprise_ai_report-2025.pdf)). The specialist strategy is not disappearing; it is migrating to self-hosted open weights, where every 2025–26 doubling-down case (Thomson Reuters, Shopify, LinkedIn, Amazon Rufus, Cisco, ServiceNow) starts from an open base and is justified by cost, latency and control, with quality claimed as "comparable" rather than "superior" outside proprietary-content tasks. The durable advantage of a specialist is cost, latency, control and data residency, not raw quality, and the Head of VI should justify each Domain-tier build on task-level wins and those operational grounds, not on beating the frontier at the domain.

### Engineering: what the published recipes say about building

This subsection is for the VI foundation team; enterprise readers can skip to the next header.

**Continued pretraining has steep diminishing returns and a better substitute.** Meditron's ~48B tokens of medical CPT on Llama-2-70B bought +1.5 to +2.4 points ([arXiv 2311.16079](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.16079.pdf)); SaulLM-7B's 30B legal tokens bought ~6 points on LegalBench-Instruct ([Equall](https://blog.equall.com/saul)); Foundation-Sec's 5.1B tokens bought +0.4 to +9 on security benchmarks and −2.4 MMLU ([arXiv 2504.21039](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21039v1)). Small-scale ablations put the plateau near 10^8.5 tokens with a decline at 10^9, and 5–10% general replay retains general knowledge ([arXiv 2510.11842](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11842v1)). Raw-corpus CPT "drastically hurts prompting ability" unless the domain text is transformed into reading-comprehension or instruction form, which is how AdaptLLM-7B matched BloombergGPT on 1.2B tokens ([AdaptLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09530.pdf)); MiST's synthetic mid-training on ~1B tokens beating 2.4B raw tokens is the 2026 confirmation ([arXiv 2609.18496](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18496)). DatologyAI's "Finetuner's Fallacy" (2026) argues that if you control pretraining, domain data mixed at 1–5% of pretraining tokens beats pretrain-then-finetune, with ~300M domain tokens giving consistent gains and 3–30M tokens hurting at long horizons; it puts the inference break-even for a smaller specialised model near 1T tokens ([arXiv 2603.16177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16177)). For a ~27B Core that already carries domain adaptation, the implication is that further domain CPT per business domain is the lowest-return lever available; synthetic rewrites of a vetted seed corpus, mixed with replay, are the higher-return one.

**Fine-tuning injects skills, format and style, not facts.** The one controlled study found RAG at 0.875 against fine-tuning at 0.504 on current-events QA, with Llama-2-7B fine-tuning dropping accuracy, and FT+RAG no better than RAG alone ([arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05934v3)). Published SFT recipes cluster at 1K–100K examples (LoRA Land mean ~72.5K, NVIDIA's "10k–100k", Lynx 2,400, TensorZero 400–700 teacher demos per task, Databricks judges from 20–30 edge cases) ([arXiv 2506.02153](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02153v2); [TensorZero](https://www.tensorzero.com/blog/distillation-programmatic-data-curation-smarter-llms-5-30x-cheaper-inference/); [VentureBeat, 4 Nov 2025](https://venturebeat.com/ai/databricks-research-reveals-that-building-better-ai-judges-isnt-just-a)). Use LoRA for instruction and task tuning (rank 256, all modules, α=2r roughly matches full fine-tuning on IFT) and full fine-tuning for any CPT, because LoRA underperforms full FT at every CPT configuration but forgets less ([Biderman et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09673v2)). Reasoning post-training (SFT on distilled traces then GRPO with verifiable rewards) is the 2025–26 recipe for mapping and extraction tasks (Fin-R1, Agentar, Foundation-Sec-Reasoning, NuExtract 3), with documented pathologies: reward hacking through empty traces, repetition on weak tasks, and a format penalty needed to stop it ([arXiv 2601.21051](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051v1)).

**Build the family by pruning and distillation; serve adapters on a frozen base; retrain adapters per base release.** NVIDIA's Minitron recipe derives a 4B from Llama-3.1-8B with ~94B tokens instead of 15T, with width pruning beating depth at ≤15B and a 1.8x family compute saving ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-prune-and-distill-llama-3-1-8b-to-an-nvidia-llama-3-1-minitron-4b-model)); Llama 3.2 1B/3B and Apple's on-device model were built the same way ([arXiv 2507.13575](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13575v3)). The de facto serving pattern (Apple, NVIDIA NIM, Predibase LoRAX, Shopify) is one frozen base per deployment target with per-feature LoRA adapters batched through a multi-LoRA engine; Apple's rule that an adapter works with only one base version means every Core refresh forces an adapter retrain, so the Core cadence must be slow or adapter training automated, as in Shopify's weekly ~12-hour full fine-tune on two H200 nodes ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/fine-tuning-agent-shopify-flow)). No public 2025–26 throughput benchmark exists for multi-LoRA serving at hundreds of adapters (gap).

**Licensing now constrains the teacher.** OpenAI's usage-policy update effective 20 Nov 2026 prohibits using its outputs to build training material, RL rubrics, model-based feedback or data filtering for models "intended as alternatives to or substitutes for OpenAI models" ([GovInfoSecurity, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/openai-tightens-rules-to-block-rival-model-distillation-a-33194)); Anthropic allows non-competing narrow models (sentiment, categorisation, summarisation, extraction, semantic search, anomaly detection) and prohibits general-purpose chatbots and "using Outputs as training targets" without written permission ([Anthropic support, 16 Mar 2026](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12326764-can-i-use-my-outputs-to-train-an-ai-model)). Synthetic-data and judge pipelines feeding Core or any assistant-style Domain model should therefore use open-weight teachers (Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek, Gemma, Mistral; licence text not re-verified in this research), with frontier APIs confined to evaluation and to generating data for narrow non-competing task heads.

## Seven task primitives recur across every domain, and none needs more than 8B

Looked at across the four candidate domains and the horizontal inventory, the same small set of task primitives recurs, and the "domain" is mostly a label on the data and rubric rather than a different capability. Compliance decomposes into classification against a policy or typology, extraction of obligations and entities, summarisation, policy Q&A with retrieval, flag-explain-rewrite pre-review, and investigation narrative drafting ([NICE Actimize](https://www.niceactimize.com/press-releases/nice-actimize-empowers-surveil-x-with-generative-ai-479/); [Hawk](https://insights.hawk.ai/agentic-ai-investigate-aml-alerts); [Finextra/Ascent](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/77489/ascent-regtech-launches-ai-driven-change-management-product)). Cybersecurity decomposes into alert and incident summarisation, triage classification, natural-language-to-query, detection-rule generation, ATT&CK and CVE→CWE mapping, CTI summarisation and incident-report drafting ([arXiv 2406.07599](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07599v3); [Microsoft RCT, Jan 2024](https://www.softcat.com/8817/1153/6619/Microsoft-Copilot-for-Security-productivity-findings-Whitepaper-Jan2024.pdf)). Advice decomposes into cited retrieval over house research, meeting transcription-to-CRM summarisation, analytics-to-narrative generation, next-best-action, and recommendation with explanation ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/); [InvestmentNews, Oct 2025](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/blackrock-debuts-ai-powered-commentary-tool-for-advisors-lands-morgan-stanley-as-first-client/262370)). The retrieval-Q&A and summarisation primitives are the ones that have demonstrably been ported across business lines (Morgan Stanley's wealth assistant became AskResearchGPT for institutional securities ([Finextra](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/44946/morgan-stanley-rolls-out-askresearchgpt-to-institutional-securities-staff)); Aladdin's commentary moved from Morgan Stanley to Wells Fargo), which is the empirical signature of a reusable capability.

Three design consequences follow. First, the Judge domain is not a business domain at all; it is the platform primitive every other domain consumes, which makes it the highest-reuse build on the list and the one with the strongest small-model evidence. Second, the Compliance and Cybersecurity "domains" reduce to the same five document-intelligence primitives (classify, extract, summarise, retrieve-and-answer, map) with thin domain-specific heads (typology scoring, SAR narrative, Sigma emission), so a shared layer with adapters serves both and also serves legal, procurement, audit and HR. Third, nothing in the horizontal set justifies a 27B model; the Core's role is the open-ended generation, agentic and narrative tasks (Shopify-class tool-calling, portfolio commentary, incident reports), and the Distil tier is where most adapters belong.

## Where the evidence argues against building, and why

**Against a trained-weight Advice model.** There is no public precedent at any regulated firm, no benchmark that scores suitability or best-interest compliance of a recommendation, and the tasks that make advice valuable (synthesis, personalised narrative, multi-step workflow, tool use) sit on the frontier side of the specialist/frontier line in every finance benchmark: FinBen found LLMs "struggle with advanced reasoning and complex tasks like text generation and forecasting" while specialists win extraction ([arXiv 2402.12659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12659)); the FIRE benchmark found exam scores above 90% collapse into the 70s on real-world scenarios and into the 60s for specialists ([arXiv 2602.22273](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22273v1)); no agent exceeds 50% on expert financial-analyst tasks ([alphaXiv 2508.00828](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2508.00828.md)); a GPT-4o agent resolved 2–4 of 12 wealth-management workflow tasks, failing mostly on data access and delivery rather than arithmetic ([arXiv 2512.02230](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02230v1)). Regulators require the suitability check to be deterministic or human, outputs to be attributable and reproducible, and client-facing text to be a supervised communication, which means the generative weights never own the regulated decision. A trained Advice model would therefore be fighting the frontier on its strongest ground, with no way to measure whether it won, for a task the model is not allowed to own. The right Advice investments are the deterministic suitability and methodology layer, the evaluation harness, and narrow adapters for narrative, summarisation and meeting capture.

**Against another domain-pretrained base (per-domain CPT at 27B).** The ~6-point gap between a 27B open model and the frontier on LegalBench is about what 540B legal tokens bought SaulLM in 2024 ([Vals AI](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legalbench)); Meditron's 48B tokens bought ~2 points; Foundation-Sec's 5B tokens cost MMLU; and the Apriel-Nemotron abstract's claim to match 32B models is unsupported on most reasoning rows ([arXiv 2508.10948](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10948v1)). Thomson Reuters, with Westlaw, Practical Law and Reuters content and a dedicated acquisition behind it, reached parity-minus-two on broad legal reasoning and won only where its retrieval had exclusive content ([LawSites, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/08/thomson-reuters-says-its-homegrown-ai-model-now-rivals-the-frontier-labs-i-take-a-closer-look.html)). BloombergGPT is unused. The Domain tier should be adapter families and synthetic mid-training on Core, not new bases.

**Against a universal judge.** Fine-tuned judges collapse out of distribution (Prometheus 2 below random on JudgeBench; Auto-J-13B at 20.9% on adversarial subsets vs GPT-4's 64.2%) and chain-of-thought hurts them while helping frontier judges ([Huang et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.306.pdf); [Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/best-llm-as-a-judge-model-reasoning-vs-fine-tuned-judges-calibration-cost-2026)). Judge adapters must be per-rubric and per-distribution, with a frontier reasoning model or panel (about $0.005 per item for a cheap three-model panel at Oct 2026 prices) kept for correctness and novel criteria. Expert inter-rater reliability of 0.3–0.6 means any judge "beating GPT-4o by 2 points" is inside label noise; deltas under ~3 points should not drive model choice.

**Against pretraining a security model.** Open 8B security models exist under permissive licences (Foundation-Sec Apache-2.0; Primus datasets ODC-BY) and were built on a few H200 nodes for ~30 hours; their honest headline is parity with GPT-4o-mini on CTI mapping and a 5–15-point deficit to 70B on breadth ([arXiv 2504.21039](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21039v1); [arXiv 2502.11191](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11191v2)). The missing ingredient for an asset manager's SOC is not knowledge but labelled operational data (its own alert dispositions, rule hits, incident write-ups), and local 7B models produced only 76% syntactically valid Sigma rules against 100% for cloud models, falling to 50% without a judge loop ([arXiv 2608.19011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19011)). Dual-use control cannot live in open weights (a decensored Foundation-Sec variant is on Hugging Face) and must live in deployment governance ([HF e12ex2](https://huggingface.co/e12ex2/foundation-sec-8b-heretic)).

**Against building commodities.** ASR, general embeddings and reranking, prompt-injection detection and the base safety taxonomy all have multiple open ≤8B options within a few points of each other; the self-host economics only favour building above roughly $10–50K per month of API spend or ~100M tokens per month, with people being 70–80% of self-hosting cost (independent blogger estimates, Apr 2026) ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/15/build-vs-buy-llm-infrastructure)).

**Against using frontier outputs as training targets for the Core.** From 20 Nov 2026 this is prohibited by OpenAI for substitute models and already prohibited by Anthropic without written permission; it is permitted for narrow non-competing task heads. Any Domain-tier synthetic pipeline must be designed around open-weight teachers from the start.

## Recommended build order: judge and compliance now, cyber next, advice last

This section is written for the VI leadership audience and states outcomes; the engineering appendix above holds the techniques. Each build carries a pre-registered hypothesis and the result that would refute it. The ordering follows three criteria from the evidence: reuse count (how many applications consume the capability), evidence strength for a small specialist, and the regulatory room to let the model own the output.

### Short term (next two quarters)

**Build 1: the Judge family on the Distil tier, as the platform's evaluation substrate.** Three adapters on one Distil base: a groundedness judge for every retrieval application, a rubric judge per business output type (advisor summary, complaint letter, incident report), and a preference reward model that feeds Core's own post-training and best-of-N serving. This is the highest-reuse build, the one with the strongest small-model evidence, and the one that makes every later build measurable; it also answers the governance direction (regulators moving beyond human-in-the-loop as sufficient, only one in five banks measuring reviewer effectiveness) by producing auditable per-output scores. Hypothesis: a 4–8B Distil judge trained on 2–5K house-labelled examples per rubric reaches agreement with expert graders within 3 points of a frontier panel on in-distribution outputs at under a tenth of the per-item cost. Refuted if, after ≥100 human labels per failure mode, the frontier panel leads by more than 3 points on the house rubric, or the judge falls below chance on a held-out adversarial set; in that case keep frontier judges and stop here.

**Build 2: house-entity PII and injection classifiers as small separate encoders.** Not glamorous, consumed by every pipeline, and the one place where the independent evidence says off-the-shelf tools fail (F1 0.48–0.63 across tools, down to 0.09 on multi-turn conversation) and a small domain fine-tune moves the number most. Hypothesis: a sub-1B token classifier fine-tuned on Vanguard-format identifiers reaches ≥0.90 F1 on house entities where open tools score below 0.65. Refuted if an open tool already clears 0.85 on the house test set, in which case adopt it.

**Build 3: Compliance pre-review and triage adapters.** Start with the two tasks that have the clearest reusable pattern and the best-evidenced gains: flag-explain-suggest-rewrite for marketing and client communications (the Saifr pattern), and alert triage scoring for communications surveillance (the Behavox and Shield pattern). Both are classification against a policy with a structured output, learnable from a few hundred to a few thousand labelled items, and both sit upstream of a human reviewer, which keeps the regulated decision where SR 26-2's "analogous governance" expects it. Hypothesis: a Distil adapter trained on house-reviewed examples reduces reviewer load by at least 40% at no loss of recall against the current process, measured on a frozen pre/post window the way HSBC measured AML. Refuted if recall on known true positives drops, or if a frontier model with the house policy in the prompt matches the adapter on the same test set, which would mean the data, not the weights, was the asset.

**Build 4: the Advice evaluation harness and deterministic suitability layer, not an advice model.** Capture the house methodology as rules and templates, build a suitability and best-interest check that runs on every generated recommendation, and assemble the benchmark the market lacks: a set of expert-graded advisor scenarios scored on methodology consistency, factual grounding and regulatory compliance of the text. Use frontier models with retrieval for generation in this period, measured by Build 1's judges. Hypothesis: the harness detects at least 90% of seeded suitability violations and methodology deviations in generated advice. Refuted if expert graders disagree with the harness on more than 20% of cases, which would mean the rubric is not yet specifiable and no model, trained or frontier, should be fed by it.

**Build 5: Cybersecurity reuse evaluation, no training.** Stand up Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning and MiST-32B alongside Core on an internal SOC task set (CTI summarisation, ATT&CK extraction, CVE→CWE mapping, Sigma generation with schema validation and a judge loop, incident-report drafting) and measure against the frontier models the SOC already has access to. Hypothesis: on mapping and extraction tasks an open 8B security model matches the frontier on the house test set at local-deployment cost; on summarisation and reports Core matches. Refuted if the frontier leads by more than 5 points on every task, in which case the Cybersecurity domain becomes a retrieval and governance project (validated rule repository, staging, evidence chains), not a model.

### Long term (12–24 months)

**Build 6: Core post-training flywheel driven by the Judge family.** Once Build 1 is calibrated, move Core's refresh onto a periodic loop: production outputs scored by house judges and expert spot checks, curated into preference and SFT data from open-weight teachers, retrained on a fixed cadence with automated adapter retraining for every Domain-tier adapter, following the weekly-retrain pattern that closed Shopify's 35% quality gap in two weeks. Hypothesis: Core's house-rubric scores improve each cycle and the family's adapter retrain cost stays under one engineer-day per release. Refuted if two consecutive cycles show no gain on held-out house evals, which would indicate the ceiling is data quality or base capability and argue for a base refresh rather than more post-training.

**Build 7: the Compliance domain as a document-intelligence adapter layer.** Extend Build 3 into the full set of compliance primitives (obligation extraction and regulatory-change mapping, KYC and contract extraction, SAR and complaint narrative drafting) as adapters on the shared layer, and expose the same primitives to legal, audit and procurement to earn the reuse count. Hypothesis: at least three non-compliance teams adopt the layer within the period, and extraction on house forms reaches parity with the best 4B open extractor or the frontier on an independent house test. Refuted if adoption outside compliance stays at zero, which would mean the "domain" was really a single application and the Domain tier should not carry it.

**Build 8: Advice narrative and meeting-capture adapters on Core, behind the suitability layer.** Only after Build 4's harness exists: a meeting-transcription-to-CRM-and-follow-up adapter (the Debrief pattern, where a fine-tuned 7B matched GPT-4 on call summarisation) and an analytics-to-narrative adapter for portfolio commentary (the Aladdin and Expert Insights pattern), with every output passing the deterministic suitability check and the Judge family. Hypothesis: Core adapters match the frontier on the house advice benchmark at lower cost and with full on-premises data residency, and advisor-graded quality equals the frontier baseline. Refuted if the frontier leads by more than 3 points on the house benchmark after the first retrain cycle; the fallback is frontier generation with house judges, which is where the whole regulated industry sits today.

**Build 9: Cybersecurity reasoning adapter, conditional on Build 5 and on volume.** If Build 5 shows the 8B open models at parity on mapping tasks and the SOC's volume justifies local serving, add house triage labels (alert dispositions, rule hits) as a Distil adapter via reasoning distillation and verifiable-reward RL, with all rule emission gated by schema validation, staging and human approval. Hypothesis: the adapter raises true-positive agreement with senior analysts above the open model's baseline by at least 10 points on house alerts. Refuted if the gain is under 5 points or the adapter's rule validity falls below the validator-gated frontier path.

The sequencing keeps the attention of a 45-person organisation on builds with the shortest path to measurable reuse: Judge and PII are consumed by everything; Compliance pre-review is the one domain where the regulated decision can sit upstream of a human and the evidence base is strongest for small specialists; Cybersecurity has open models to inherit; and Advice, the domain with the greatest strategic pull, is the one where the evidence most clearly says to build the measurement and the guardrail first and the model last.

## Gaps the research could not close

The following gaps should be read as conditions on the recommendations above. **No independent benchmark exists for any production advisor copilot or SOC copilot**; every outcome figure from Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Behavox, Smarsh, Shield and Verafin is firm- or vendor-reported, and CrowdStrike's "40 hours per week" is an estimate of alerts times five minutes rather than a measurement ([CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/agentic-ai-innovation-in-cybersecurity-charlotte-ai-detection-triage/)). **No benchmark anywhere scores the suitability or best-interest compliance of an LLM recommendation for a US retail client**; the nearest attempts are Ant's Finova compliance benchmark (tables not retrieved) and a Mar 2026 life-cycle paper whose results were paywalled ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/llms-as-financial-advisors-for-individuals/)). **No controlled study compares a shared-base adapter family against separately trained models** on judge, guardrail or extraction quality, or measures interference between adapters; the per-primitive size figures come from separate papers on different benchmarks. **No public 2025–26 multi-LoRA serving benchmark** at hundreds of adapters was found. **All enterprise cost and TCO figures are company- or blogger-reported**; no audited study exists, and Thomson Reuters, Walmart and Amazon publish no cost numbers. **Thomson-1-Large and Databricks TAO have no third-party verification.** Several vendor model sizes (Behavox, Luminance, Stripe, Mastercard, Visa, Intuit, Wells Fargo's PII model, SentinelOne's "Ultraviolet") are undisclosed, and Cisco's own papers disagree on Foundation-Sec's training-token count (5.1B vs "8B proprietary"). **No bank has published the approval file its validators required for an in-house LLM**, and OCC and Fed guidance specific to generative AI after SR 26-2 is still pending. The primary OpenAI usage-policy text and the output-use terms of the Llama, Qwen and DeepSeek licences were not re-verified. Finally, search budgets ran out before Goldman Sachs' own compliance AI, ESMA and BaFin positions after May 2024, insurer and asset-manager SOC statements, FinBen leaderboard scores for current small specialists, and FinGPT's numeric results could be checked, and no Vanguard-internal material was searched; Vanguard appears only through its public press.

## Conclusion

The landscape resolves into a single rule that the Head of VI can apply to every Domain-tier proposal: a trained specialist earns its place by owning a narrow, structured, high-volume task on data the frontier cannot see, and loses its place the moment it is asked to be a domain. The firms that look like exceptions confirm it. Thomson Reuters built a legal model from the richest proprietary legal corpus in the world and reached parity-minus-two except where retrieval was exclusive; Bloomberg built a finance model from the richest proprietary finance corpus and does not use it; the firms whose models survived (Stripe, Mastercard, Nubank, Capital One, Shopify, LinkedIn, Cisco) built them for tasks the frontier structurally cannot do or cannot do cheaply. For an organisation that already owns a 27B Core, this reframes the Domain tier from "four domain models" into "one evaluation substrate, one document-intelligence layer, and a disciplined set of adapters", with the Core's value concentrated in open-ended narrative and agentic work and the Distil tier carrying nearly everything else.

The second-order implication is about what an internal team will actually want and reuse. The capabilities that have been ported across business lines in the public record are retrieval-with-citations, summarisation and analytics-to-narrative, and the capabilities every application consumes silently are judges, guardrails and PII handling. The Domain tier that earns demand counts is the one that packages those primitives with the house rubric, the house policy and the house entity formats, because that is the only layer of "domain" the evidence shows moving the number. Advice, the domain with the most strategic pull, is where the measurement does not yet exist, and building that measurement is the only move that makes any later Advice model, trained or rented, defensible to a regulator or to the advisors who will be asked to trust it.
