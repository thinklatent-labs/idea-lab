Glossary

The words we use, in plain words.

Forty-odd terms, one line each, kept by the blog editors. Perspective posts link here instead of explaining; if a term needs more than a line, it belongs in a Deep dive.

Suggest a term
List web part · filter by letter:ACDEFGHIJLMNOPQRSUVW
AgentSoftware that uses a model to take steps toward a goal, such as answering a client or filing a form. At ThinkLatent, agents propose; people decide.
AI GatewayThe one door every model call at the Enterprise goes through. It registers each usage and logs calls as each use case's approval requires.
AI PerspectivesThinkLatent's monthly seminar: a paper, what it means at the Enterprise, the ThinkLatent solution that carries it, and your requests for the roadmap.
CoreThe TLM4 tier that knows the Enterprise best: trained on curated Enterprise data; the 27B is the accuracy reference, the 7B the fast one.
Decision modelA fast yes-or-no, classification or extraction model with a confidence score, for agents that must decide in milliseconds.
DistilThe TLM4 tier for high-volume, bounded tasks: open architectures trained on data distilled from Core, cheaper and faster.
DistillationTeaching a smaller model from a larger one's outputs so it keeps most of what matters at a fraction of the cost.
DomainA TLM4 specialist built on Core for one business domain with a queue of use cases: Advice, Compliance, Judge, Cybersecurity.
DriftA model's results slowly changing in production as the world or the data changes. Monitoring watches for it.
EndpointThe address a team's software calls to use a model. ThinkLatent gives each use case its own.
EvaluationA test of a model on a fixed set of tasks, scored the same way every time, so releases can be compared.
Foundation modelA large model trained on broad data that other models and products are built on. TLM Core is the Enterprise's.
FP8A compact number format that lets a model run faster and cheaper on the same hardware, with care taken to keep accuracy.
Frontier modelThe largest general models from the big vendors. ThinkLatent uses them where they fit, with the Enterprise's own model in front.
GenerationA major TLM release line: TLM1 to TLM5. Each is a different bet, told in outcomes on the Products page.
GroundingMaking a model answer from given, checkable sources rather than from memory alone.
GuardrailA rule or model that stops an answer that must not be given, before it reaches a person.
HallucinationA confident answer that is not true. Grounding, evaluation and human review are the defences.
HarnessThe fixed set-up a model runs in for a use case: prompts, tools, checks. The index can score a harness, not only a model.
Human reviewStep five of How we build responsibly: reviewers from the partner team check outputs at the level and frequency the approval sets.
HyperPodThe compute cluster TLM is trained and served on.
Independent validationStep four of How we build responsibly: the Enterprise's model risk management checks a model independently before it is approved. ThinkLatent supplies the evidence and does not sign.
Index runOne complete scoring of models on the TL Index, dated and numbered. Every ThinkLatent number names its run.
InferenceUsing a trained model to answer. Latency and cost at inference are part of the mandate.
IntakeStep one of How we build responsibly: a team brings a problem through the form or the portal and it becomes a recorded request; the model is registered in the Enterprise's model inventory.
Judge modelA model used to score other models' answers in an evaluation. Its own accuracy is checked against people.
LatencyHow long an answer takes. The desk model answers while the client is still on the line.
M-cubeA red-teaming method published by ThinkLatent's Research: a way to attack a model before an attacker does.
Model cardThe short record every model carries: what it is for, who uses it, status, limits in plain words, owner. The full card is in the ThinkLatent Portal.
Model inventoryThe Enterprise's record of every model and its approval and validation status. Where this site and the inventory differ, the inventory is right.
MonitoringStep six of How we build responsibly: calls logged as the approval requires; drift, refusals and escalations watched; a use case below its bar is reported to Risk.
RelayThe SDK developers use to reach TLM: Python and Node packages that follow the client patterns they already know.
Omni modelA model that works across text, speech and images rather than one of them.
Perspective · Deep diveThe two kinds of post on this site: a Perspective for business readers, outcomes only; a Deep dive for practitioners, technique allowed.
PruningRemoving parts of a model that contribute little, so it runs smaller and faster.
QuantizationStoring a model's numbers in a smaller format, such as FP8, to run cheaper with little loss.
Red teamingDeliberately attacking a model to find what it gets wrong or lets through, before launch.
Self-healingA ThinkLatent method that finds a model's domain and skill gaps and generates or locates the data to close them.
SLMA small language model. Often enough for a bounded task; ThinkLatent can tell from a team's data whether one will work before training.
SufficiencyThe index's headline: whether a model is good enough for the Enterprise's skills, against a target, with an interval.
Use caseOne job for one team done with a ThinkLatent model, recorded in the portal with its status, business unit and one number.
The TL IndexThe TL Index: how models, ours and others, score on the skills the Enterprise needs.
TLMTLM line: the models that carry the Enterprise's knowledge, in five generations so far.
ThinkLatent PortalOne place to find, try, train and run ThinkLatent's models, and to bring a problem.
How we build responsiblyThinkLatent's six steps for a use case, alongside the Enterprise's model risk management, which approves and validates.
World modelA model that learns from observation to predict where an interaction is going. TLM5 is one.
