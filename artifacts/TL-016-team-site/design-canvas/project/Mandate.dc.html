The mandate

The Enterprise's intelligence must compound.

Our mandate is to build the AI that is infused with the Enterprise's proprietary data: language models, speech, omni and thinking models, foundation models, whatever the work needs. One intelligence for the Enterprise, built with a long-run view.

How we deliver itThe proof so far

Why our own models

Everyone who uses a vendor's AI gets as good as the vendor. No better.

A general frontier model is built for everyone. It does not carry what the Enterprise knows, and it is not built to take care of the particular things the Enterprise's business needs: the feedback loop from our customers' voice, the way our advice has to be given, the risk we are not allowed to take.

Where is that feedback loop? How do we get better from what our customers tell us? Only a model that is ours can learn from it. That is why ThinkLatent builds models infused with the Enterprise's proprietary data, and why they sit in front of whatever frontier model we also use.

Compounding

Frontier intelligence compounds. Does the Enterprise's?

01

The expert in the loop

Every frontier solution compounds the expert knowledge that flows into it. The question for us is whether the Enterprise's intelligence, the business intelligence we hold, is compounding at the same rate.

02

If it is not, nothing goes further

Over the next two, three, five years, a business whose own intelligence does not compound stands still while everyone else moves. In the worst case that is existential.

03

Assets under management are not the moat

Startups will stand up. What stops one of them becoming the Enterprise is not the size of the book; once it starts draining in fifty-five directions we are not in the game. The moat is what we know, compounding.

Scale

Ten tasks today, a hundred tomorrow, by the same people.

The biggest problem in this industry is scale: giving every client the same quality of service with people behind the wheel is not possible. An augmented workforce can do it, and to do it we need our own model sitting in front of the work as well.

The game with AI is not taking somebody's job. It is helping the person who can do ten tasks today do a hundred in the same time. That is the scale we are after, and it is why latency and cost are part of the mandate, not afterthoughts.
