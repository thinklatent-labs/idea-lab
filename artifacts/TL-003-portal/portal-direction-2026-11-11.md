# ThinkLatent Portal (portal) — Direction

11 Nov 2026 · TL-003 portal · set by Jithin, Head of ThinkLatent (D-236) · prototype version 1794380010-0793, portal c177eae

## Why we build it

To express the collective intelligence of the organisation, at institution level, by embedding it in models. No frontier lab offers that to one enterprise. Once it is embedded, teams use the models for new use cases; those use cases generate interaction data and feedback beyond the first documents; that data makes the next version better. The business intelligence compounds.

We say this when we present. The portal does not say it: people should look at the portal and see it compounding.

## What follows from that

1. **Models and use cases, not roles.** The model is the core pillar: a foundation model, a domain model built from it, and the use case on top. Versions move together: when a foundation model gets a new version, each domain model on it is due one.
2. **People attach to a use case only.** A member adds someone by company email; the portal sends the link. Members see and do more inside their use case. There is no other role.
3. **Open to everyone.** What the Head sees, everyone sees: every use case, its model, hosting, usage, spend, value and point of contact, as a leaderboard. Teams adopt when they can see what other teams are doing.
4. **Like a frontier lab inside the company.** A team gets the model, dedicated or shared hosting, training, direct use and the engineering behind the API, in one place.
5. **Every number links** to the page behind it. Flows and graphs must mean something: what is built on what, what feeds what.
6. **Every request is a signal.** One request fans out into several calls; each gives signals. Capture them and show them. A number shown in the portal is the requirement for the backend that must produce it.
7. **The data loop is a product.** The system finds kinds of data its training sets lack (entity extraction plus classification) and shows them. Application teams can share their users' ratings back. Both feed the next dataset.
8. **The prototype is the full requirement for the next year.** Build everything; do not cut for timeline, roadmap or role. If it is too much for someone, the answer later is an option to hide, not leaving it out.
9. **AI runs it.** Onboarding is a conversation with an agent. A guide on every page answers from the records, shows the chart, links to the page and can page a person.

## What the prototype shows today

- Command centre: key numbers, leaderboard, model-to-use-case flow, paid and avoided, what is open, the loop and signals one click away.
- A space for every use case; Advisor summary carries the full sample (quality by day, the trial, the record, cost).
- Catalogue of foundation models; each model's page lists the use cases on it and the versions trained from it.
- TL Index: every model's score, best model per pillar, a model picker, then the detailed tables, arena, pillar pages.
- The loop: one turn followed record by record. Signals: what one request gives, and what is not captured yet.
- Data loop: new kinds of data found, feedback shared back.

## Open

- A value index for the leaderboard: no formula agreed. Today it ranks by value avoided and shows each use case's share of tasks.
- Only Advisor summary has daily series; the other use cases show what their record holds.
- Pillars proposed for a later TL Index edition are listed unscored; the TL Index (TL-004) decides.
- How much of each version's gain came from new data rather than a new foundation model is not measured.
