A helper is an agent sent off by another to do one part of the work. In the session it is one folded line. Opened, it shows what it was asked, its own steps and what it sent back. You read a helper's thread; you write to the agent that sent it.

3 · Several helpers at once

Two work at a time

An agent may send up to two helpers at once; more wait their turn. Each helper counts as one request against your limit today, and the header keeps the tally.

Research helper sent 3 helpers · two at a time1 of 3 done · 4 requests so far Read the three fact sheetsHelper 1 · 14 seconds · 4 stepsDone Check the arithmeticHelper 2 · step 2 of about 4Working Look up each fund's share classesHelper 3 · step 1 of about 3Working

Helpers do not see each other's work or your other sessions. Each has its own context; only what it sends back is added to the session's.

4 · Add a message while it works

Steering

While an agent is working through its steps, you can add to what it is doing. The agent reads your message before its next step; nothing it has done is lost. It counts as one request.

While a plain answer is being written, with no steps, there is nothing to steer: your message waits and is sent when the answer ends, as the board for the writing box says.

5 · Stop it

All of it, or one helper

Stop ends the agent and every helper it sent. A helper can also be stopped alone, and the agent carries on without that part and says so. What was done stays in the session, marked as stopped.

7 · If you leave the page

The work carries on

Work that needs nothing from you carries on when you go to another page or close the window. When you come back, the session shows what happened while you were away, and where it is now.

9 · In your inbox

When it ends, and when it waits

Work you were not watching puts one line in the inbox when it ends, and one at once when it needs your yes or no. The line opens the session at that point. The answer is given there, where the steps can be seen, not in the inbox.

No line is added when you were on the session as it finished. A line that needs you always counts on the bell; a "done" line follows your choice in You.

10 · What cannot run in the background

It waits instead

Leaving the page never gives an agent more freedom. Anything that needs you stops and waits, whether you are there or not.

A tool that changes somethingWaits for "allow this once", every timeWaits A question from the agentWaits for your choice or your own wordsWaits A connector that needs you to sign in againWaits; the rest of the work that does not need it carries onWaits A run or a write in a code workspaceWaits for your yes to each oneWaits Reading, searching, calculating, writing the answerCarries on, up to 25 steps in one answerCarries on

A schedule is different: nobody is there at all, so a tool that changes something is left out rather than waited for.

11 · When a helper cannot finish

The agent says so in its answer

A helper that fails does not fail the whole answer. The agent carries on with what it has and says which part is missing and why.
